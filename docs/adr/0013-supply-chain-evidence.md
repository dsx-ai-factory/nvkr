- **Relates to:** ADR-05 (evidence linkage), ADR-09 (provenance), ADR-01 (canonical codec / serialization
  profile), ADR-19 (fleet boundary).

## Context

The evidence contract includes per-artifact SBOMs, external references for acquired artifacts, and deferred
vulnerability results. The set is deterministic, bound to artifact identity, and folds into the evidence bundle
(ADR-05). One boundary governs all of it: NKD **produces** evidence and does not operate the fleet.

## Decision

### 1. SBOM identity: per-artifact + aggregate index

Emit a **deterministic CycloneDX SBOM per built artifact**, bound to that artifact's digest, plus an **aggregate
index** linking them. Serialization is deterministic — the serial number derives from the build/artifact
identity, with **no timestamps and no random UUIDs** — so documents are byte-identical across runs and validate
and ingest cleanly into stock scanners. Publisher-generated SBOM identity is also **mirror-invariant**: source,
toolchain, and prebuilt components use stable semantic identifiers plus their exact content pins. A repository
hostname, registry prefix, mirror key, or other relocatable acquisition locator cannot perturb the per-artifact
SBOM, its aggregate index, or ADR-05's publisher-evidence digest while the content and build identities are
unchanged.

This identity rule applies to evidence **generated by the publisher from its qualified producer report**. It does
not erase the location of separately acquired upstream evidence, which is an authenticated acquisition record
governed by §2: the transport locator is preserved so an auditor can retrieve or mirror the declared bytes, and a
separate digest authenticates those bytes. Moving that external reference changes the acquisition declaration
even when the verified payload digest is unchanged; relocating the publisher's build inputs does not change
locally generated evidence identity.

### 2. External evidence for acquired artifacts

A `prebuilt` (no-compile) component carries a **verified reference** to the upstream's external SBOM/provenance.
The reference is a canonical transport location (HTTPS, OCI, or a mirror key); the content digest is a separate
exact identity, and the URL need not embed it. The referenced bytes **must hash to the declared digest or fail
closed**. Where no upstream evidence exists, the component declares an explicit **`evidence: none`** — never a
silent absence. That is **distinct from a Tier-4 exclusion** (`fromSource:false` plus a reason). One owning
schema defines the reference, consumed by both the acquire lane and the SBOM emitter.

`evidence: none` is an honest statement about an acquisition. It is **not evidence and never satisfies an
evidence-required promotion gate**. If release policy permits a missing evidence class, the exception is an
explicit, authenticated waiver naming the exact artifact digest, missing evidence types, policy
digest, reason, approver identity, and expiry. The waiver is policy evidence; it does not masquerade as an SBOM
or provenance receipt. Waiver issuance and release evaluation are not implemented.

### 3. Vulnerability-report determinism

A **shelled** Apache-2.0 scanner (grype/trivy — subprocess, never linked) produces a report naming the **scanned
artifact, the pinned scanner DB/policy version, and the build identity**, with **scan-time omitted**. The
DB/policy version is an **explicit pinned input**: a "deterministic" report only holds if the moving DB is
captured as a pin. A missing scanner **fails closed**, or is print-only under an explicit flag. A de-risking
spike covers this (below).

### 4. Advisory → affected-build boundary

Advisory generation and vulnerability scanning are outside the current product scope. The unused advisory,
scanner-argv and release-only vulnerability receipt implementations are removed. Pinned Syft composition
inspection in §6 remains implemented; it does not evaluate vulnerabilities or grant release clearance.

### 5. Qualified release evidence receipts — deferred

The maintained verifier recomputes same-process SBOM and provenance from exact qualified producer bytes and
compares their digests with an authenticated ValidationVector. External-reference acquisition still checks
exact payload bytes, schema and artifact subject before adding SBOM properties or recording explicit gaps.
These operations do not assemble release clearance. The unused external-evidence receipt adapters and release
waiver subsystem are removed.

A future qualified release gate must accept evidence only through opaque, exact-byte capabilities or the
signed receipts below. Same-process capabilities cannot be reconstructed from a decoded report or a boolean;
release policy must bind each input to its exact artifact, evidence type, schema and policy.

Any evidence crossing a persistence, process, registry, or organizational boundary uses a canonical,
digest-addressed receipt:

```
EvidenceReceipt {
  schemaVersion
  receiptType              # sbom | provenance | vulnerability | external
  artifactDigest           # exact subject bytes
  evidenceDigest           # exact canonical evidence bytes
  evidenceSchema
  verificationPolicyDigest
  producerIdentity
  verdict                  # accepted | rejected
}
```

The boundary-crossing receipt is detached-signed and authenticated against the trust policy for its
`receiptType`. The verifier recomputes both subject and evidence digests before issuing or accepting it. A
same-process capability MUST NOT be serialized as a substitute for this receipt; if it must outlive or leave its
issuer process, it is promoted into the signed form first.

A path, transport URL, mutable tag, untyped boolean, decoded report, or caller assertion such as `--verified` is
neither form. A qualified capability or receipt for one artifact, evidence document, schema, or policy cannot
satisfy another. Component-level references are not implicitly reusable across produced artifacts: every receipt
names the exact artifact subject it covers. The deferred ADR-16 release gate must consume only those capabilities or the
authenticated receipt set; raw evidence files remain the auditable payloads those receipts reference.

The removed evidence-gap waiver implementation used `nkd.dev/evidence-waiver/v3`. Its digest family remains
reserved and nonminting; no current reader or issuer accepts this as clearance. A future waiver protocol must
bind component, build, artifact, builder-profile scope, status checkpoint, missing evidence types, policy,
approver and validity interval. It must authenticate its own schema and signer policy without reinterpreting
older signed bytes or reintroducing deployment roles.

### 6. Pipeline SBOM and acquisition scope

V1 emits deterministic CycloneDX documents per observed component artifact and an aggregate index through a
per-build SBOM node after terminal observation. Each subject is the runner-observed artifact digest; the index
also binds Build Digest. Resolved component declarations are frozen in the plan so the node needs no live
catalog. Adding those declarations or outputs changes canonical plan identity and requires regeneration.
The internal index uses `nkd.dev/sbom-index/v2`; generated CycloneDX properties bind `nkd:buildDigest`.
Replacing the former Recipe binding changes document identity; old evidence is not silently translated.

A Definition may select a catalog-pinned Syft executable through `sbomTool`. Both maintained NKE
Environments require it. The graph then requires each observed payload and the acquired scanner as explicit
SBOM inputs. Missing payloads, missing scanner pins, unsupported formats, mismatched bytes, scanner errors and
empty inventories fail closed. Without `sbomTool`, the existing declaration-only documents provide source
metadata; they do not claim dependency inspection.

A `disk-image` admitted for execution or publication requires a full CycloneDX inventory whose subject is the
exact delivered QCOW2 payload digest. It combines an authenticated trace of every fetched or built material with
read-only inspection of the final disk, then reconciles both views. The material collector, disk codec/inspector,
and package scanner are pinned tools and explicit Plan inputs. Missing tools or trace members, unreadable
filesystems, unsupported encryption, scanner failure, or disagreement fails closed at admission or inspection.
A catalog declaration may validate without those tools, but it is not a disk SBOM and cannot compile or publish.
Disk Plan lowering, material tracing, inspection, reconciliation, and evidence publication are not implemented
by the authoring schema.

This reconciliation proves the composition observed in one exact disk; it is not a reproduction comparison.
Matching dependency digests, installed-package sets, selected file hashes, or SBOM documents across two Runs can
support content validation and diagnose drift, but cannot mint `Reproduced` when the qualified artifact subject
differs. Each different QCOW2 digest remains a new Artifact identity with its own SBOM subject and production
provenance. Independently reproduced or acquired materials keep their own evidence relationships without
upgrading the disk that consumed them.

A narrower, opt-in local installed-package diagnostic may join the factory's live staged-material trace to
read-only inspection before the issuing Run closes. Factory accepts only its original held Packer output and
an admitted concrete inspection runtime. The runtime binds an exact OCI closure and inspector/codec identities;
capacity admission precedes the additional anonymous disk snapshot. The runtime owns admission, snapshot
creation, scanning, snapshot closure and permit release as one cancelable, joined operation; no borrowed
snapshot or capacity reservation escapes to a caller. The package-issued, process-local result
binds the original Run owner, named output, Plan, Build, QCOW2 digest/size and inspected runtime/tool bytes.
Equal subject bytes or Build identity cannot authorize reuse by a sibling attempt. Closing the Run cancels
active inspection, joins cleanup and revokes live use, including shallow receipt copies. Historical installed
metadata remains inspectable but cannot be serialized and restored as authority. Omitting this opt-in does not
claim inspection. Opting in makes successful inspection a condition of local Run completion: a failed scan,
subject check or cleanup fails the Run and discards its uncommitted disk export. A failed Run exposes no
installed inventories. This prevents a requested inventory from being paired with undelivered or differently
observed bytes; it does not grant publication or qualification authority. This diagnostic is not the complete acquisition trace, reconciliation, full CycloneDX
document, disk publication gate or qualification described above.

For binaries and OCI image exports, the SBOM owner invokes the pinned scanner as an owned, bounded subprocess
inside the offline utility container. Inputs are held and checked against producer digests. Syft's file resolver
requires a pathname, so binary inspection uses a private read-only copy of held bytes and checks it again before
acceptance; OCI inspection consumes the held descriptor directly. Scanner configuration and environment are
isolated. The report is projected into deterministic component names, versions and package URLs. File-location
records, temporary paths, timestamps and scanner-generated IDs are excluded. Scanner executable identity is
recorded separately. Unknown versions remain unknown; they are never filled from unrelated module tags.

For Helm source archives and exact acquired archives selected as Helm outputs, a generic manifest reader inventories bundled `Chart.yaml` declarations and chart
dependency declarations/locks. It never executes
installation scripts or fetches external charts. Bundled charts and locked references are
distinguished; a locked reference does not establish the referenced chart's transitive contents. These
documents describe the observed archive before image-binding/package transformations. Raw artifacts
are admitted to this reader only when the compiled output, acquisition producer, and resolved component
all identify the same acquired Helm chart; other raw formats remain unsupported.

Per-artifact documents keep the subject in `metadata.component` and use `components` for discovered packages
or declared chart dependencies. Stable `bom-ref` values distinguish versions with the same name. Root dependency
edges cover the inventory; they assert artifact inclusion/reference, not invented package-to-package call graphs.
The index binds the populated document digest. Hosted finalization rejects missing dependency inventories and
wrong subject/scanner bindings when inspection was requested. This is package discovery, not vulnerability scanning
or qualified reproduction. It does not claim dependencies absent from artifact metadata can be discovered.

SBOMs assert composition. Packaging requires each exact output's SBOM set and producer record
as explicit graph inputs. The shared packaging/finalization validator checks exact output, Build Digest, subject, document
and requested scanner identities. Per-build indexes are internal handoffs only. The authoritative release index
owns published evidence membership (ADR-20 §6); every required document is referenced once in its owning artifact repository and carries explicit
subject kind/digest and stage. Byte-identical inventories may be published once after every independent output
handoff passes validation. Publication deduplication cannot replace an output's missing producer record or evidence.
CLI evidence covers the built binary. Native-image evidence targets its exact manifest and records the verified
OCI-export input used by the scanner. Helm source evidence remains bound to the source archive; a separate
packaging inventory reads the final bound archive after image binding and checksum refresh. That inventory
covers chart declarations/references, not an execution or scan of referenced image contents.

Each production document is published only inside a CycloneDX attestation in a Sigstore v0.3 bundle.
An explicit pre-publication job creates and verifies those bundles with the configured workflow identity,
issuer and transparency policy. Bundles are OCI referrers in their owning output repositories; an association
to the final Helm manifest never changes the source-archive subject of source evidence. The release signature
covers their exact manifest/blob identities and the canonical document digests through release metadata.
Independent consumers verify the bundle's statement subject and signature. No extra signature on the SBOM
wrapper or raw published document is required.
Consumers derive mandatory evidence slots from output membership and reject missing, duplicate, substituted
or unbound documents and stages. Finalization compares the published documents with the validated source
handoffs and final package inventory. The current consumer has no old-layout fallback. This establishes
pipeline artifact-publication evidence, not qualified receipts, vulnerability clearance or signed completion.

SBOMs remain `static-unqualified` in dry-run. Generation alone is no signature or reproduction proof. Acquired components retain `evidence: none` unless pinned upstream evidence is declared. V1 does not
verify upstream signatures/provenance on acquisition. Vulnerability scanning stays outside v1.
Curated/embedded aggregates use dependency edges from aggregate to members (ADR-10/22).

## Consequences

**Positive**
- Every built artifact has deterministic, digest-bound SBOM and (via ADR-09) provenance an auditor can recompute.
- Acquired artifacts are honestly marked — verified reference or `evidence: none` — preserving the
  from-source XOR prebuilt XOR exclusion trichotomy.
- Promotion distinguishes present evidence, absent evidence, and an explicitly authorized waiver without
  converting absence into success.
- Vulnerability results are reproducible when the DB/policy is pinned, and consumable by stock scanners.

**Negative / costs**
- Vuln-report determinism requires provisioning and mirroring a pinned scanner DB/policy — an infra dependency
  for air-gap.
- External-evidence conventions vary per upstream; some references degrade to `evidence: none`.
- Persisted and cross-boundary receipt issuers and waiver approvers require explicit trust-policy roles and
  key-rotation procedures; same-process capabilities require careful private-state and mutation-isolation tests.

**Neutral**
- All evidence serializes through the canonical codec (ADR-01) and feeds the evidence bundle (ADR-05).

## Alternatives considered

- **A single aggregate SBOM keyed on the build.** Rejected — FR-15 requires per-built-artifact SBOMs; it blocks
  artifact and inventory consumers.
- **Link a scanner library.** Rejected — staying Apache-clean requires shelling the scanner.
- **Maintain a released-build index to map advisories to the fleet.** Rejected — it crosses the
  produce-vs-operate boundary.

## De-risking spikes

1. **Vuln-report determinism** — two runs over a fixture SBOM plus a pinned DB snapshot are byte-identical,
   offline.

## Follow-ups

- **ADR-05:** the SBOM and provenance digests are the per-artifact evidence rows the bundle aggregates.
- **ADR-09:** provenance is the companion evidence per built artifact.
- **ADR-16:** derives the exact required receipt types from Inventory plus promotion policy, and rejects
  `evidence: none` unless an exact, unexpired waiver receipt is authorized.
- **ADR-22 §7 / ADR-10:** the aggregate SBOM's dependency graph points from an aggregate to its members when
  they come from a curated pin set or from a `wrapped-build`'s `embeds` (aggregate → members), rather than
  presenting them as unrelated peers; the CycloneDX representation of that edge is this ADR's to fix.

## Acceptance evidence

Current module evidence in `pkg/nkd/sbom/*_test.go`, `pkg/nkd/attest/*_test.go`, and
`pkg/nkd/catalog/prebuilt_test.go` covers deterministic documents, prebuilt declarations, publisher evidence
equality across online/mirror transport relocation, same-process exact-byte qualification, HTTPS-reference byte
and subject verification, and mutation rejection. It does not prove vulnerability scanning, waiver evaluation,
release clearance or signed cross-process receipt publication.

Acceptance additionally requires a boundary fixture that promotes same-process capabilities into canonical
signed receipts, publishes and reloads them, recomputes the exact artifact/evidence bytes, rejects receipt-type
and signer-role confusion, rejects replay to another artifact or policy, proves `evidence: none` fails an
evidence-required promotion, and proves only an exact unexpired waiver signed by the configured waiver authority
permits the documented exception.
